Shop and Currency System

A. Overview​

Merchant Bella sells potions, bread and a piece of armour in the village square. She has her own gold, her own stock and her own prices, and the hero arrives with a purse of their own. A purchase moves gold from the hero to the merchant, moves an item from the merchant to the pack, and refuses to happen when one of the three sides cannot deliver.

DifficultyIntermediate
ComplexityHigh — two documents, a stock catalog, a transaction with three checks and a lock
Fusion skillsFrame events, Active objects, Counter and String objects, global variables, comparison expressions
JSON++ skillsAbsolute paths, loops over an object, searches, arithmetic on two documents, file work, error handling

Gameplay scenario​

The player walks into the village square and meets Bella. The panel of the shop opens and lists what she sells, with the price and the remaining stock of every line. Buying a health potion costs twenty-five gold. Buying with a purse that holds less is refused with a message, and the purse and the stock of the shop keep the values they had. Selling a potion to Bella pays twelve gold, half of what she asks.

Learning objectives​

By the end of this guide you will be able to:

  • describe the stock of a shop as an object whose members are the identifiers of the items;
  • read the price and the stock of a line without a search, because the path is built from the identifier;
  • check the funds of the hero, the stock of the shop and the room of the pack before a transaction;
  • refuse a transaction that one of the three sides cannot carry out;
  • move the gold and the item in a fixed order, and lock the shop while the transaction runs;
  • sell an item to the merchant at a rate that the shop decides;
  • save the state of the shop and of the purse.

What you will build​

The merchant NPC, the panel of the shop with its lines, the purse of the hero, the stock and the gold of Bella, the buy and sell events, and the two files shop.json and inventory.json.

B. Prerequisites​

Fusion knowledge you need

  • Frame events such as Start of Frame and Start of Level.
  • Active objects, the is clicked condition and the is overlapping Active condition.
  • The Counter object and the Set counter action.
  • The String object and the Set alterable string action.
  • Global variables on the Special object, written in this manual as Special : Set <name> to <value>.
  • Comparison conditions, written as value1 < value2 or value1 >= value2.

JSON++ concepts you need

  • JSON paths, because the price of a line is read from a path built with the identifier of the item.
  • JSON instance state, because this guide uses two JSON++ Object instances that hold two documents and never see each other's content.
  • Understanding JSON Loops and reading loop values, because the panel of the shop is drawn with a loop over the stock.
  • Missing, null and empty, because a line that has no stock and a line that does not exist are different situations.
  • Search results and their lifetime, because the sell flow checks the pack of the hero with a search.

Runtime limitations to keep in mind

  • A transaction that touches two documents is not atomic. If the second write fails, the first one has already happened. This guide keeps the number of writes small and checks everything before the first write.
  • File paths follow the host. Apppath$ + "shop.json" works on Windows. HTML5 needs a browser reference such as storage://shop, and the mobile targets write inside the sandbox of the application. See platform-specific file operations.
  • A save emits On JSON Saved to File when the file work completes. On some targets the completion is asynchronous, so the confirmation belongs in that event.

Standard objects used here

JSON++ Object, Counter, String, Special, Active and Timer. Nothing else is required.

Related prerequisite tutorials

  • Stackable Inventory System, which builds the pack of the hero and the scan that this guide calls.
  • Equipment and Character Stats, which stores the prices of the items in the catalog.
  • Manage a Simple Inventory
  • Search for Keys and Values

C. Gameplay Scenario​

The village square of Emberfall has one merchant. Bella stands next to her cart, and the player meets her when the hero walks close enough. The panel of the shop opens on top of the scene and lists four lines: a health potion, a mana potion, a bread and a leather armour.

The merchant keeps two documents. The first one, shop.json, holds her stock, the price of every line, the gold of her chest and the rate at which she buys from the hero. The second one, inventory.json, holds the catalog of the items and the record of the hero, with the purse and the pack. The two documents are held by two JSON++ Object instances, so the events of the shop and the events of the pack never overwrite each other.

A purchase is a transaction with three parties. The hero must have enough gold, Bella must have the item in stock, and the pack must have a free slot. The events check the three conditions first, and they write only when all three hold. The order of the writes never changes, and the shop stays locked until the item has reached the pack.

D. Final Result​

The finished system behaves like this:

  • The panel opens when the hero meets Bella and lists every line with its price and its remaining stock.
  • Buying a health potion removes twenty-five gold from the purse, adds twenty-five gold to the chest of the merchant, removes one potion from the stock and puts the potion in the pack.
  • Buying with less than twenty-five gold is refused with the message Not enough gold, and nothing changes.
  • Buying a line that Bella does not have is refused with the message The shop has no stock.
  • Buying when the pack has no free slot is refused with the message The pack is full.
  • Clicking the buy button twice does not charge the hero twice: the shop stays locked until the transaction ends.
  • Selling a potion to Bella pays twelve gold, and the potion leaves the pack.
  • Selling something the hero does not carry is refused with a message.
  • The purse, the chest of the merchant and the stock of every line are refreshed on the panel after each transaction.
  • The state of the shop and the state of the hero are written to their files, and a failed file operation produces a readable message.

E. JSON Data Structure​

The document of the shop​

{
  "schema_version": 1,
  "shop": {
    "npc": "npc_merchant_bella",
    "name": "Bella's Supplies",
    "gold": 800,
    "buy_rate": 0.5,
    "stock": {
      "potion_health": {"name": "Health Potion", "price": 25, "stock": 4},
      "potion_mana": {"name": "Mana Potion", "price": 30, "stock": 2},
      "bread": {"name": "Bread", "price": 8, "stock": 10},
      "armor_leather": {"name": "Leather Armor", "price": 90, "stock": 1}
    }
  }
}

The members of the shop​

MemberTypeMeaning
npcstringThe identifier of the merchant. It tells the game which NPC runs the shop.
namestringThe name of the shop, shown on the title of the panel.
goldnumberThe gold of the merchant. It grows when the hero buys and shrinks when the hero sells.
buy_ratenumberThe share of the price that the merchant pays when the hero sells. A rate of 0.5 means half of the price.
stockobjectThe lines of the shop. The members of this object are the identifiers of the items.

The lines of the shop​

The stock is an object whose members are the identifiers of the items, so the price of a line is a direct path.

MemberTypeMeaning
namestringThe name of the item, shown on the line of the panel.
pricenumberThe price of one unit for the hero.
stocknumberThe number of units that the merchant still has.
What is readPathExample
The price of a line/shop/stock/<id>/price/shop/stock/potion_health/price
The stock of a line/shop/stock/<id>/stock/shop/stock/potion_health/stock
The name of a line/shop/stock/<id>/name/shop/stock/potion_health/name

Because the identifier is the name of the member, the events of the shop never need a search to find a line. The identifier of the chosen line is enough to build every path.

The document of the hero​

The second document is the one built by the stackable inventory guide. The shop needs three parts of it:

{
  "schema_version": 1,
  "items": {
    "potion_health": {"name": "Health Potion", "type": "potion", "stack": 10, "value": 25},
    "potion_mana": {"name": "Mana Potion", "type": "potion", "stack": 10, "value": 30},
    "bread": {"name": "Bread", "type": "food", "stack": 5, "value": 8},

  "player": {
    "name": "Ari",

    "capacity": 12,

G. Step-by-Step Implementation​

Step 1 — Create the frame and the objects​

  1. Create a frame named Village Square and place Bella and the panel on it.
  2. Add the objects of the previous section.
  3. On the Special object, create the global variables Chosen Id (string), Bought Amount (number), Unit Price (number), Total Price (number), Player Gold (number), Shop Stock (number), Pack Slots (number), Pack Capacity (number), Refusal Reason (number), Purchase Requested (number), Trade Lock (number) and Delivery Pending (number). Leave them at 0, except Chosen Id, which starts empty.

Step 2 — Load the two documents​

Add a Start of Frame event with two actions:

  • Action: Shop Data : Load JSON from file at Apppath$ + "shop.json".
  • Action: Inventory Data : Load JSON from file at Apppath$ + "inventory.json".

Two files, two instances, two independent documents. A failure in one of them reaches the On Error event of Step 16.

Step 3 — Draw the lines of the panel​

Add an event with the condition Shop Data: On JSON Loaded:

  • Action: Shop Data : Start loop "Stock" at "/shop/stock".

The stock is an object, so the loop walks its members. Add an event with the condition Shop Data: On Loop "Stock":

  • Action: Row Name Label : Set alterable string to GetString( "Shop Data", GetLoopPath( "Shop Data", "Stock" ) + "/name" ).
  • Action: Row Price Label : Set alterable string to Str$(GetNumber( "Shop Data", GetLoopPath( "Shop Data", "Stock" ) + "/price" )).
  • Action: Row Stock Label : Set alterable string to Str$(GetNumber( "Shop Data", GetLoopPath( "Shop Data", "Stock" ) + "/stock" )).

The path of the current member comes from Get Loop Path, and it already carries the identifier of the line: /shop/stock/potion_health. The name, the price and the stock are read under that path. A panel with several lines uses one set of labels per line, and the index of the loop tells the game which line to fill.

Step 4 — Open the panel when the hero meets the merchant​

Add an event with the condition Player is overlapping Merchant Bella:

  • Action: Shop Panel : Set visible.

And an event with the condition Close Button is clicked:

  • Action: Shop Panel : Set invisible.

The condition is overlapping Active is checked every frame, so the panel opens as soon as the hero walks close enough to Bella.

Step 5 — Remember the line that the player chooses​

Add one event per line of the panel. The first one reads:

  • Condition: Row 1 Button is clicked.
  • Action: Special : Set Chosen Id to "potion_health".
  • Action: Row Name Label : Set alterable string to GetString( "Shop Data", "/shop/stock/potion_health/name" ).
  • Action: Row Price Label : Set alterable string to Str$(GetNumber( "Shop Data", "/shop/stock/potion_health/price" )).

The second line uses the identifier bread and its own button. The click does not change any document. It only records the choice and refreshes the labels of the panel.

Step 6 — Read the numbers of the transaction​

Add an event with the condition Buy Button is clicked:

  • Action: Special : Set Bought Amount to 1.
  • Action: Special : Set Unit Price to GetNumber( "Shop Data", "/shop/stock/" + Chosen Id + "/price" ).
  • Action: Special : Set Total Price to Unit Price * Bought Amount.
  • Action: Special : Set Player Gold to GetNumber( "Inventory Data", "/player/gold" ).
  • Action: Special : Set Shop Stock to GetNumber( "Shop Data", "/shop/stock/" + Chosen Id + "/stock" ).
  • Action: Special : Set Pack Slots to GetNumItems( "Inventory Data", "/player/inventory" ).
  • Action: Special : Set Pack Capacity to GetNumber( "Inventory Data", "/player/capacity" ).
  • Action: Special : Set Refusal Reason to 0.
  • Action: Special : Set Purchase Requested to 1.

The numbers are read from the two documents and copied into global variables, so the conditions of the next steps are short and readable. Nothing is written yet.

Step 7 — Check the three conditions of the transaction​

Under the event of Step 6, add a group with the condition Trade Lock = 0, and inside it a group with the condition Player Gold < Total Price:

  • Action: Special : Set Refusal Reason to 1.

Add a second group under the same outer group, with the condition Shop Stock < Bought Amount, and inside it a group with the condition Refusal Reason = 0:

  • Action: Special : Set Refusal Reason to 2.

And a third group under the same outer group, with the condition Pack Slots >= Pack Capacity, and inside it a group with the condition Refusal Reason = 0:

  • Action: Special : Set Refusal Reason to 3.

The three groups are siblings, so every condition is checked. The inner guard Refusal Reason = 0 keeps the first condition that failed, so a purchase with an empty purse is reported as Not enough gold and never as a missing stock.

Step 8 — Refuse the purchase with a readable message​

Add an event with the condition Refusal Reason > 0:

  • Condition of the group: Refusal Reason = 1.

  • Action: Status Text : Set alterable string to "Not enough gold".

  • Action: Special : Set Purchase Requested to 0.

  • Action: Special : Set Refusal Reason to 0.

  • Condition of the group: Refusal Reason = 2.

  • Action: Status Text : Set alterable string to "The shop has no stock".

  • Action: Special : Set Purchase Requested to 0.

  • Action: Special : Set Refusal Reason to 0.

  • Condition of the group: Refusal Reason = 3.

  • Action: Status Text : Set alterable string to "The pack is full".

  • Action: Special : Set Purchase Requested to 0.

  • Action: Special : Set Refusal Reason to 0.

Place this event after the event of Step 9. The flag Purchase Requested returns to 0, so the next click starts a new attempt. The order matters: the event of Step 9 tests the reason, and the reason must still be there when that event runs.

Step 9 — Apply the transaction​

Add an event with the condition Purchase Requested = 1, placed after the event of Step 6 and before the event of Step 8, and a group with the condition Refusal Reason = 0:

  • Action: Special : Set Trade Lock to 1.
  • Action: Inventory Data : Subtract number Total Price at "/player" with key "gold".
  • Action: Shop Data : Add number Total Price at "/shop" with key "gold".
  • Action: Shop Data : Subtract number Bought Amount at "/shop/stock/" + Chosen Id with key "stock".
  • Action: Gold Counter : Set counter to GetNumber( "Inventory Data", "/player/gold" ).
  • Action: Shop Gold Counter : Set counter to GetNumber( "Shop Data", "/shop/gold" ).
  • Action: Row Stock Label : Set alterable string to Str$(GetNumber( "Shop Data", "/shop/stock/" + Chosen Id + "/stock" )).
  • Action: Special : Set Delivery Pending to 1.
  • Action: Special : Set Wanted Id to Chosen Id.
  • Action: Special : Set Purchase Requested to 0.
  • Action: Inventory Data : Start loop "Inventory" at "/player/inventory".

The lock is set before the first write and is released only when the item has reached the pack. The order of the writes never changes: the hero pays first, the merchant is paid second, and the stock is reduced last. The loop that starts the delivery is the scan of the pack, which comes from the stackable inventory guide, and the identifier of the bought item is stored in Wanted Id so that the scan finds the stack that may hold it.

Step 10 — Deliver the item to the pack​

Add an event with the condition Inventory Data: On End Loop "Inventory" and a group with the condition Delivery Pending = 1:

  • Condition of the group: Found Flag = 1.
  • Action: Inventory Data : Add number Bought Amount at Entry Path with key "quantity".
  • Action: Status Text : Set alterable string to "The purchase was made".
  • Action: Special : Set Delivery Pending to 0.
  • Action: Special : Set Trade Lock to 0.

And a second group with the condition Found Flag = 0:

  • Action: Inventory Data : Insert object "{""id"":""potion_health"",""quantity"":1}" at "/player/inventory" with key "entry".
  • Action: Status Text : Set alterable string to "The purchase was made".
  • Action: Special : Set Delivery Pending to 0.
  • Action: Special : Set Trade Lock to 0.

The scan of the pack stores the path of the stack that holds the identifier in the global variable Entry Path, and sets Found Flag to 1 when it finds it. The first branch adds the units to that stack, the second opens a new one. The literal JSON text is the example of this guide; a general implementation builds it from the identifier and the amount with the Fusion string concatenation.

Step 11 — Sell a stack to the merchant​

Add an event with the condition Sell Button is clicked:

  • Action: Special : Set Sell Amount to 1.
  • Action: Special : Set Sell Price to Floor(GetNumber( "Inventory Data", "/items/" + Chosen Id + "/value" ) * GetNumber( "Shop Data", "/shop/buy_rate" )).
  • Action: Special : Set Wanted Id to Chosen Id.
  • Action: Special : Set Sell Pending to 1.
  • Action: Inventory Data : Start loop "Inventory" at "/player/inventory".

Add the global variables Sell Amount (number), Sell Price (number) and Sell Pending (number) on the Special object. The global variable Wanted Id is the one that the scan of the pack reads, and the two globals Found Flag and Entry Path are the ones that the scan fills.

The price that the merchant pays is a share of the value of the item in the catalog, and the share comes from the document of the shop. The rounding is the work of Fusion, and the expression Floor turns a share of twelve and a half into twelve.

Step 12 — Pay the hero and take the stack​

Under the same On End Loop event, add a group with the condition Sell Pending = 1, and inside it a group with the condition Found Flag = 1:

  • Action: Inventory Data : Subtract number Sell Amount at Entry Path with key "quantity".
  • Action: Inventory Data : Add number Sell Price at "/player" with key "gold".
  • Action: Shop Data : Subtract number Sell Price at "/shop" with key "gold".
  • Action: Gold Counter : Set counter to GetNumber( "Inventory Data", "/player/gold" ).
  • Action: Shop Gold Counter : Set counter to GetNumber( "Shop Data", "/shop/gold" ).
  • Action: Status Text : Set alterable string to "The item was sold".
  • Action: Special : Set Sell Pending to 0.

And a second group with the condition Found Flag = 0:

  • Action: Status Text : Set alterable string to "The hero has nothing to sell".
  • Action: Special : Set Sell Pending to 0.

The merchant must be able to pay. A shop with less gold than the price of the item refuses the sale, and the check is added as a group with the condition GetNumber( "Shop Data", "/shop/gold" ) < Sell Price and the message The merchant cannot pay.

Step 13 — Refresh the panel after a transaction​

The counters of the purse and of the chest are updated by the actions of Steps 9 and 12. The labels of the stock of the chosen line are updated by the same events. The names and the prices of the lines do not change, so they are read once by the loop of Step 3.

Step 14 — Save the state of the shop and of the hero​

Add an event with the condition Save Button is clicked:

  • Action: Shop Data : Save JSON to file at Apppath$ + "shop.json".
  • Action: Inventory Data : Save JSON to file at Apppath$ + "inventory.json".

Add a second event with the condition Shop Data: On JSON Saved:

  • Action: Status Text : Set alterable string to "The shop was saved".

And a third one with the condition Shop Data: On Error:

  • Action: Status Text : Set alterable string to GetError( "Shop Data" ).

The two files are written one after the other, and the second write is not undone when the first one fails. When the state of the shop and the state of the hero must stay consistent, save them in the same file of the run, as the multiple save slots guide does.

Step 15 — Restore the shop when the level starts​

Add an event with the condition Start of Level:

  • Action: Shop Data : Load JSON from file at Apppath$ + "shop.json".
  • Action: Inventory Data : Load JSON from file at Apppath$ + "inventory.json".

The stock of the merchant and the purse of the hero come back from the files, and the loop of Step 3 draws the panel again.

Step 16 — Handle the errors of the two documents​

Add a second On Error event for the document of the hero:

  • Condition: Inventory Data: On Error.
  • Action: Status Text : Set alterable string to GetError( "Inventory Data" ).

The two error branches are separate because the two documents fail for different reasons: the file of the shop may be missing on a fresh installation, while the file of the hero may be corrupt.

J. Edge Cases & Error Handling​

SituationWhat happensHandling
shop.json does not existThe load fails and the On Error event of the shop runsWrite the file with the game on the first run, and offer the default stock.
inventory.json does not existThe load fails and the On Error event of the hero runsThe shop must refuse every purchase while the purse is unknown, because a missing purse would read as zero.
The shop has no stock memberThe loop of Step 3 finds nothing and the panel stays emptyCheck /shop/stock with the exists condition before the loop, and validate the file against a JSON schema.
The chosen identifier is not a line of the shopThe price reads zero and the purchase is refused by the check of the stockNever leave the identifier of a line in a global that the events do not reset.
The hero has exactly the price of the lineThe comparison is true and the purchase goes throughUse a comparison that accepts the equality, as this guide does.
The merchant has no goldThe sale is refused and the item stays in the packCompare the gold of the merchant with the price before paying the hero.
The pack is fullThe purchase is refused before any writeCompare the number of stacks with the capacity before the first write.
The buy button is clicked twice in the same frameThe second click is ignored while the shop is lockedKeep the lock until the delivery ends, as this guide does.
The scan of the pack finds no stackA new stack is openedThe delivery has two branches, one for each case.
The pack changes while the transaction runsThe path of the stack may point at another entryThe lock keeps the shop closed until the delivery ends.
The save of the shop failsThe first file may already be writtenKeep the confirmation in On JSON Saved to File, and write the two documents of the same run in one file.
The game runs on HTML5Apppath$ has no meaningUse a browser reference such as storage://shop and read the platform-specific file guide.
The game runs on Android or iOSThe files land in the sandbox of the applicationUse the path documented for that target and test the export.

Two rules deserve emphasis. First, a transaction checks everything before it writes anything. Second, the lock is released by the delivery, not by the click, so a second click never pays twice.

K. Runtime Compatibility​

The system relies on four groups of features: file operations, loops over an object, searches, and arithmetic on two documents.

TargetFile workLoops, searches and valuesNotes
WindowsImplemented and testedImplemented and testedApppath$ resolves to the folder of the exported application.
AndroidImplemented, not verified by an automated checkImplemented, not verified by an automated checkWrites inside the sandbox of the application. Test the export.
HTML5Implemented and testedImplemented and testedUse a browser reference. storage:// uses the origin storage, download:// dispatches a download, embedded:// is read-only.
macOSUnknownUnknownNo separate build was verified.
iOSImplemented, not verified by an automated checkImplemented, not verified by an automated checkWrites inside the sandbox of the application. Test the export.

A second difference matters for the counters of the interface. The number of items of a collection reads the size of the collection on the native runtime and returns zero for a value that is not a collection on HTML5. The pack is always an array, so both targets agree. The full matrix is on the runtime compatibility page.

M. Next Steps​

  • Give every line a picture. The catalog of the shop can hold a member with the name of the image, and the panel reads it with the same path.
  • Add a second merchant. A second shop document and a second JSON++ Object instance are enough; the events of the buy and the sell are the same, and the identifier of the merchant chooses the document.
  • Let the hero repair the shop. A repair action adds gold to the chest of the merchant and raises the stock of every line, using the loop of Step 3.
  • Show a discount. A member of the shop can hold the rate of the discount, and the price of the panel is the price of the line minus the discount.
  • Keep the two files safe. The extension can encrypt the file it writes, so the stock of the merchant and the purse of the hero are not readable as plain text. See Enable Encryption.
