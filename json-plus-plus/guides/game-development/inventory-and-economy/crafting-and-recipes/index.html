Crafting and Recipes

A. Overview​

Ari gathers lotus herbs, iron ore and bone shards in the forest and carries them to the alchemy table of the village. The recipes are written down, not programmed: each one names the result it produces and the ingredients it consumes. The events of Fusion check whether the hero carries the materials, take them away, and put the result in the pack.

DifficultyAdvanced
ComplexityHigh — two documents, three recipes, two working tables and four sequential loops
Fusion skillsFrame events, Active objects, String objects, global variables, comparison expressions
JSON++ skillsAbsolute paths, loops over an object, writes with a dynamic key, arithmetic, file work, error handling

Gameplay scenario​

The player opens the panel of the alchemy table and chooses the recipe of the health potion. The panel shows the two lotus herbs and the one bone shard that the recipe needs, and compares them with what the hero carries. When a material is missing, the table says so and nothing is consumed. When everything is there, the herbs and the shards leave the pack and a health potion appears in it.

Learning objectives​

By the end of this guide you will be able to:

  • describe a recipe with a result, a list of ingredients and the quantity that each ingredient needs;
  • read the requirements of a recipe with paths built from the identifier of the recipe;
  • compare the requirements with what the hero carries;
  • consume the ingredients and create the result of the recipe;
  • refuse a craft that the hero cannot pay for in materials;
  • craft several units of the same recipe at once;
  • keep the recipes in a document that the game loads and saves.

What you will build​

The panel of the alchemy table, three recipes of Emberfall, the scan that measures the pack of the hero, the check of the requirements, the consumption of the ingredients and the file crafting.json.

B. Prerequisites​

Fusion knowledge you need

  • Frame events such as Start of Frame.
  • Active objects and the is clicked condition.
  • The String object and the Set alterable string action.
  • Global variables on the Special object, written in this manual as Special : Set <name> to <value>.
  • Comparison conditions, written as value1 < value2 or value1 >= value2.

JSON++ concepts you need

  • JSON paths, because the requirements of a recipe are read with a path built from the identifier of the recipe and of the ingredient.
  • Understanding JSON Loops and reading loop values, because the ingredients of a recipe are an object and the loop walks its members.
  • Loop lifecycle, because this guide runs several loops one after the other and each one has its own name.
  • Missing, null and empty, because an ingredient that the hero does not carry is a missing member of the working table, not a member with a quantity of zero.
  • JSON instance state, because the recipes and the pack of the hero live in two documents and the events copy values from one to the other.

Runtime limitations to keep in mind

  • A loop works on a snapshot. The loops of this guide run one after the other, and none of them runs while another one is running.
  • The key of a write accepts an expression, so a member can be created with the identifier of an item as its name. The identifier must be a valid member name: letters, digits and underscores.
  • File paths follow the host. Apppath$ + "crafting.json" works on Windows. HTML5 needs a browser reference such as storage://crafting, and the mobile targets write inside the sandbox of the application. See platform-specific file operations.

Standard objects used here

JSON++ Object, String, Special, Active and Timer. Nothing else is required.

Related prerequisite tutorials

  • Stackable Inventory System, which builds the pack of the hero and the file that holds it.
  • Shop and Currency System, which moves items and gold between two documents.

C. Gameplay Scenario​

The alchemy table of the village stands under a roof in the corner of the square. The player walks to it and opens the panel of the recipes. Three recipes are known to Ari: the health potion, the mana potion and the iron sword. Each of them is a member of the document of the crafting, named by its identifier.

A recipe holds the result it produces and the ingredients it consumes. The ingredients are an object whose members are the identifiers of the items, so the recipe of the health potion needs two units of herb_lotus and one unit of bone_shard. Because the ingredient is the name of the member, the events read the requirement of an ingredient with a path built from two identifiers.

The pack of the hero is an array of stacks, and an array does not answer the question "how many units of this ingredient does the hero carry" with a single path. The craft therefore starts by measuring the pack: a loop walks the stacks and writes two working tables, one with the quantity of each item and one with the path of the stack that holds it. The check of the requirements and the consumption of the ingredients then work with those two tables, one loop after the other.

The order of the craft never changes. The pack is measured, the requirements are compared, the ingredients are taken away and the result is created. A step that fails stops the craft before the ingredients are taken, so a failed craft never costs the hero anything.

D. Final Result​

The finished system behaves like this:

  • The panel lists the three recipes with the result they produce.
  • Choosing a recipe shows its name, its result and the ingredients it needs.
  • Crafting the health potion with four lotus herbs and two bone shards works: the herbs drop to two, the shards drop to one and a health potion appears in the pack.
  • Crafting with one lotus herb instead of two is refused with the message Not enough materials, and the pack keeps every item it had.
  • Crafting consumes the materials exactly once, whatever the number of ingredients of the recipe.
  • Crafting four potions at once consumes four times the materials and produces four potions.
  • A potion that the hero already carries joins the existing stack instead of opening a second one.
  • A full pack refuses the craft before any material is taken.
  • The recipes are written to their file and the pack is written to its own file.

E. JSON Data Structure​

The craft uses two documents, because the recipes belong to the game and the pack belongs to the hero. The file of the pack is the one built by the stackable inventory guide.

The document of the recipes​

{
  "schema_version": 1,
  "recipes": {
    "potion_health": {
      "name": "Health Potion",
      "result": {"id": "potion_health", "quantity": 1},
      "ingredients": {"herb_lotus": 2, "bone_shard": 1}
    },
    "potion_mana": {
      "name": "Mana Potion",
      "result": {"id": "potion_mana", "quantity": 1},
      "ingredients": {"herb_lotus": 1, "ore_iron": 1}
    },
    "sword_iron": {
      "name": "Iron Sword",
      "result": {"id": "sword_iron", "quantity": 1},
      "ingredients": {"ore_iron": 3, "bone_shard": 2}
    }
  }
}

The recipes​

The recipes are an object whose members are the identifiers of the recipes. The path of a recipe is built from its identifier, so the requirements of the health potion are read under /recipes/potion_health.

MemberTypeMeaning
namestringThe name of the recipe, shown on the panel.
resultobjectThe item that the recipe produces. It holds the identifier of the item in id and the number of units in quantity.
ingredientsobjectThe materials of the recipe. The members are the identifiers of the items and the values are the numbers of units that the recipe needs.
What is readPathExample
The name of a recipe/recipes/<id>/name/recipes/potion_health/name
The result of a recipe/recipes/<id>/result/id/recipes/potion_health/result/id
The quantity of the result/recipes/<id>/result/quantity/recipes/potion_health/result/quantity
The requirement of an ingredient/recipes/<id>/ingredients/<ingredient>/recipes/potion_health/ingredients/herb_lotus

The ingredients​

The ingredients of a recipe are an object, and not an array, because the identifier of the ingredient is the name of the member. Two consequences follow. The requirement of an ingredient is read with a direct path, and a loop over the ingredients gives the identifier of the current ingredient with Get Loop Key.

Ingredient of potion_healthRequired unitsIn the pack at the start
herb_lotus24
bone_shard12

The result of a recipe​

The result is an object with two members, and it uses the same identifier as the items of the pack.

MemberTypeMeaning
idstringThe identifier of the item that the recipe produces, such as potion_health.
quantitynumberThe number of units that one craft produces. A recipe of a potion produces one unit.

The identifier of the result is the identifier of an item of the pack, so the result joins an existing stack when the hero already carries that item. The scan of Step 6 looks for it and stores the path of the stack in Entry Path.

The document of the hero​

    "bone_shard": {"name": "Bone Shard", "type": "material", "stack": 20, "value": 4},
    "potion_health": {"name": "Health Potion", "type": "potion", "stack": 10, "value": 25}
  },
  "materials": {},
  "material_paths": {},
  "player": {
    "name": "Ari",
    "level": 3,
    "gold": 250,
    "capacity": 12,

MemberTypeMeaning
items/<id>/valuenumberThe price of the item in the catalog. The shop guide uses it.
player/goldnumberThe purse of the hero, for the recipes that cost money.
player/capacitynumberThe number of slots of the pack.
player/inventoryarrayThe stacks of the pack, in the shape built by the stackable inventory guide.

The working tables​

Two members of the document of the hero are working tables. They are rebuilt at the beginning of every craft and they hold the measurement of the pack.

MemberTypeMeaning
materialsobjectThe quantity of each item of the pack, with the identifier of the item as the name of the member.
material_pathsobjectThe path of the stack that holds each item of the pack, with the identifier as the name of the member.

The tables exist because the pack is an array. An array answers how many stacks are there with a single path, but it does not answer how many units of this item are there with a single path, because the identifier of the item is a value inside the entries and not a name of a member. The scan of Step 6 turns the array into two tables that the rest of the craft can read with plain paths.

The tables are working state. They are rebuilt at every craft, and the game may delete them when the craft is over.

The four loops of the craft​

The craft runs several loops, one after the other, and each one has its own name.

LoopDocument and pathWhat it does
PanelCrafting Data, /recipes/<id>/ingredientsDraws the ingredients of the chosen recipe.
PackInventory Data, /player/inventoryMeasures the pack and fills the two working tables.
IngredientsCrafting Data, /recipes/<id>/ingredientsCompares every requirement with the working table.
ConsumptionCrafting Data, /recipes/<id>/ingredientsTakes the units away from the stacks of the pack.

The names matter, because every condition and every expression of a loop uses the name of the loop it belongs to. Two loops over the same path with different names are two different runs; the same name is the same run.

The loop of the panel and the loop of the check read the same collection, and they never run at the same time. They still carry different names, because they read the collection with different intentions and because a name is the only thing that tells the two runs apart.

G. Step-by-Step Implementation​

Step 1 — Create the frame and the objects​

  1. Create a frame named Alchemy Table and place the table on it.
  2. Add the objects of the previous section.
  3. On the Special object, create the global variables Recipe Id (string), Ingredient Id (string), Needed Amount (number), Available Amount (number), Missing Ingredient (number), Craft Count (number), Wanted Id (string), Found Flag (number), Entry Path (string), Pack Slots (number) and Pack Capacity (number). Leave them at 0, except the two text ones, which start empty.

Step 2 — Load the two documents​

Add a Start of Frame event with two actions:

  • Action: Crafting Data : Load JSON from file at Apppath$ + "crafting.json".
  • Action: Inventory Data : Load JSON from file at Apppath$ + "inventory.json".

The recipes and the pack of the hero come from two files. A failure reaches the On Error event of Step 16.

Step 3 — Choose a recipe​

Add one event per recipe of the panel. The first one reads:

  • Condition: Recipe 1 Button is clicked.
  • Action: Special : Set Recipe Id to "potion_health".
  • Action: Recipe Name Label : Set alterable string to GetString( "Crafting Data", "/recipes/" + Recipe Id + "/name" ).
  • Action: Recipe Result Label : Set alterable string to GetString( "Crafting Data", "/recipes/" + Recipe Id + "/result/id" ) + " x " + Str$(GetNumber( "Crafting Data", "/recipes/" + Recipe Id + "/result/quantity" )).

The other two events use the identifiers potion_mana and sword_iron. The click only records the choice; the craft itself happens on the button of Step 5.

Step 4 — List the ingredients of the chosen recipe​

Under the event of Step 3, add one action:

  • Action: Crafting Data : Start loop "Panel" at "/recipes/" + Recipe Id + "/ingredients".

And an event with the condition Crafting Data: On Loop "Panel":

  • Action: Special : Set Ingredient Id to GetLoopKey( "Crafting Data", "Panel" ).
  • Action: Ingredient Label : Set alterable string to GetString( "Inventory Data", "/items/" + Ingredient Id + "/name" ) + " x " + Str$(GetNumber( "Crafting Data", "/recipes/" + Recipe Id + "/ingredients/" + Ingredient Id )).

Because the ingredients are an object, the loop gives the identifier of the current ingredient with Get Loop Key, and the requirement is read with a path that ends with that identifier.

Step 5 — Prepare the craft​

Add an event with the condition Craft Button is clicked:

  • Action: Special : Set Craft Count to 1.
  • Action: Special : Set Missing Ingredient to 0.
  • Action: Special : Set Found Flag to 0.
  • Action: Special : Set Wanted Id to GetString( "Crafting Data", "/recipes/" + Recipe Id + "/result/id" ).
  • Action: Special : Set Pack Slots to GetNumItems( "Inventory Data", "/player/inventory" ).
  • Action: Special : Set Pack Capacity to GetNumber( "Inventory Data", "/player/capacity" ).
  • Action: Inventory Data : Insert object "{}" at "/" with key "materials".
  • Action: Inventory Data : Insert object "{}" at "/" with key "material_paths".

The two working tables are emptied before every craft, so a table of a previous craft never answers a question of the current one. The identifier of the result is stored in Wanted Id, because the scan of Step 6 looks for the stack that holds it.

Under the same event, add a group with the condition Pack Slots >= Pack Capacity:

  • Action: Status Text : Set alterable string to "The pack is full".

Step 6 — The scan measures the pack​

Under the Craft Button event from Step 5, add a group with the condition Pack Slots < Pack Capacity:

  • Action: Inventory Data : Start loop "Pack" at "/player/inventory".

The click starts the scan after clearing the working tables. Every craft measures the current pack before checking ingredients. Add an event with the condition Inventory Data: On Loop "Pack":

  • Action: Inventory Data : Set number GetNumber( "Inventory Data", GetLoopPath( "Inventory Data", "Pack" ) + "/quantity" ) at "/materials" with key GetString( "Inventory Data", GetLoopPath( "Inventory Data", "Pack" ) + "/id" ).
  • Action: Inventory Data : Insert string GetLoopPath( "Inventory Data", "Pack" ) at "/material_paths" with key GetString( "Inventory Data", GetLoopPath( "Inventory Data", "Pack" ) + "/id" ).

The first action writes the quantity of the current stack under the identifier of its item. The second writes the path of that stack under the same identifier. The key of the write is an expression, so the identifier of the item becomes the name of the member.

Under the same event, add a group:

  • Condition of the group: Wanted Id = GetString( "Inventory Data", GetLoopPath( "Inventory Data", "Pack" ) + "/id" ).
  • Action: Special : Set Entry Path to GetLoopPath( "Inventory Data", "Pack" ).
  • Action: Special : Set Found Flag to 1.

The group remembers the stack that holds the result of the recipe, so the result can join it instead of opening a second stack.

Step 7 — Start the check of the requirements​

Add an event with the condition Inventory Data: On End Loop "Pack":

  • Action: Crafting Data : Start loop "Ingredients" at "/recipes/" + Recipe Id + "/ingredients".

The check reads the requirements from the document of the recipes and the available amounts from the working tables of the document of the hero.

Step 8 — Compare the requirement with what the hero carries​

Add an event with the condition Crafting Data: On Loop "Ingredients":

  • Action: Special : Set Ingredient Id to GetLoopKey( "Crafting Data", "Ingredients" ).
  • Action: Special : Set Needed Amount to GetNumber( "Crafting Data", "/recipes/" + Recipe Id + "/ingredients/" + Ingredient Id ) * Craft Count.
  • Action: Special : Set Available Amount to 0.

Under that event, add a group with the condition Inventory Data : Is "/materials/" + Ingredient Id exists:

  • Action: Special : Set Available Amount to GetNumber( "Inventory Data", "/materials/" + Ingredient Id ).

And a second group with the condition Available Amount < Needed Amount:

  • Action: Special : Set Missing Ingredient to 1.
  • Action: Status Text : Set alterable string to "Not enough materials".
  • Action: Crafting Data : Stop loop "Ingredients".

The existence test comes first, because an ingredient that the hero does not carry has no member in the working table. The Stop Loop action ends the check at the first shortage, so the recipe does not report a list of ten missing materials when three are missing.

Step 9 — Refuse the craft when a material is missing​

Add an event with the condition Crafting Data: On End Loop "Ingredients", and a group with the condition Missing Ingredient = 0:

  • Action: Crafting Data : Start loop "Consumption" at "/recipes/" + Recipe Id + "/ingredients".

The loop of Step 8 was stopped when a material was missing, so the flag Missing Ingredient is 1 in that case and the consumption never starts. A craft that cannot be paid for leaves the pack untouched.

Step 10 — Consume the ingredients​

Add an event with the condition Crafting Data: On Loop "Consumption":

  • Action: Special : Set Ingredient Id to GetLoopKey( "Crafting Data", "Consumption" ).
  • Action: Special : Set Needed Amount to GetNumber( "Crafting Data", "/recipes/" + Recipe Id + "/ingredients/" + Ingredient Id ) * Craft Count.
  • Action: Inventory Data : Subtract number Needed Amount at GetString( "Inventory Data", "/material_paths/" + Ingredient Id ) with key "quantity".

The path of the stack comes from the working table of the paths, and the action subtracts the units from the member quantity of that stack.

Step 11 — Create the result​

Add an event with the condition Crafting Data: On End Loop "Consumption", and a group with the condition Found Flag = 1:

  • Action: Inventory Data : Add number Craft Count at Entry Path with key "quantity".
  • Action: Status Text : Set alterable string to "The recipe was crafted".

And a second group with the condition Found Flag = 0:

  • Action: Inventory Data : Insert object "{""id"":""potion_health"",""quantity"":1}" at "/player/inventory" with key "entry".
  • Action: Status Text : Set alterable string to "The recipe was crafted".

The literal JSON text is the example of this guide. A general implementation builds that text from the identifier of the result and from the number of units, with the Fusion string concatenation:

"{""id"":""" + GetString( "Crafting Data", "/recipes/" + Recipe Id + "/result/id" ) + """,""quantity"":""" + Str$( GetNumber( "Crafting Data", "/recipes/" + Recipe Id + "/result/quantity" ) * Craft Count ) + """}"

Step 12 — Craft several units at once​

The number of units to craft is a global variable, and it multiplies every requirement of the recipe. Add an event with the condition Count Button is clicked:

  • Action: Special : Set Craft Count to Craft Count + 1.
  • Action: Craft Count Label : Set alterable string to Str$(Craft Count).

The multiplication happens in Step 8 and in Step 10, where the requirement is multiplied by Craft Count. The result of the first group of Step 11 adds the same number of units to the existing stack.

Step 13 — Keep the pack clean​

The consumption only subtracts the units. A stack that reaches zero stays in the array with a quantity of zero, because a deletion would move the entries that come after it and the paths of the working table would no longer match the document. The stackable inventory guide removes the entries that are empty, and the craft can call the same rule once the craft is over.

Step 14 — Save the recipes and the pack​

Add an event with the condition Save Button is clicked:

  • Action: Crafting Data : Save JSON to file at Apppath$ + "crafting.json".
  • Action: Inventory Data : Save JSON to file at Apppath$ + "inventory.json".

Add a second event with the condition Crafting Data: On JSON Saved:

  • Action: Status Text : Set alterable string to "The recipes were saved".

The two files are written one after the other, and the second write is not undone when the first one fails.

Step 15 — Restore the recipes when the level starts​

Add an event with the condition Start of Level:

  • Action: Crafting Data : Load JSON from file at Apppath$ + "crafting.json".
  • Action: Inventory Data : Load JSON from file at Apppath$ + "inventory.json".

The next Craft Button click starts the scan from Step 6 and refreshes the working tables.

Step 16 — Handle the errors​

Add an event with the condition Crafting Data: On Error:

  • Action: Status Text : Set alterable string to GetError( "Crafting Data" ).

And a second one for the document of the hero:

  • Condition: Inventory Data: On Error.
  • Action: Status Text : Set alterable string to GetError( "Inventory Data" ).

A craft that starts before the documents are loaded has no recipe to work with. The events of the panel check the existence of /recipes before they start a craft.

After a refused craft​

Nothing changes. The message reads Not enough materials, and the pack keeps four herbs and two shards.

The working tables are left as the scan filled them, because the scan of the pack is the last step that ran. The next craft empties them before it measures the pack again, so the tables never answer a question with a measurement of an earlier craft.

J. Edge Cases & Error Handling​

SituationWhat happensHandling
crafting.json does not existThe load fails and On Error runsWrite the file with the game on the first run and offer the three recipes of the panel.
The document has no recipes memberThe loop of Step 4 finds nothing and the panel stays emptyCheck /recipes with the exists condition before the craft, and validate the file against a JSON schema.
The chosen identifier is not a recipeThe requirement reads zero and the craft produces an empty resultKeep the identifier of the recipe in a global that only the buttons of the panel write.
The hero carries none of an ingredientThe member is absent from the working tableTest the existence of the member before reading it, as this guide does.
The hero carries fewer units than the recipe needsThe check stops the craft and the message names the shortageCompare the available amount with the needed amount for every ingredient.
The pack has no room for the resultThe craft is refused before the materials are takenCompare the number of stacks with the capacity before the scan, as Step 5 does.
An ingredient stack reaches zeroThe entry stays in the array with a quantity of zeroRemove the empty stacks after the craft; a deletion during the craft would move the entries and invalidate the paths of the working table.
The player crafts the same recipe twiceThe second craft rebuilds the working tables and consumes the materials againEmpty the tables at the beginning of every craft, as this guide does.
Two crafts overlapThe loops of the two crafts share their namesKeep the table closed while a craft runs, with a flag that the button of the craft checks.
The identifier of an item is not a valid member nameThe write with a dynamic key failsUse identifiers made of letters, digits and underscores, and validate the file.
The recipe produces an item the hero already carriesThe result joins the existing stackThe scan of Step 6 stores the path of that stack, and the first branch of Step 11 adds the units to it.
The recipe needs an ingredient the hero carries in two stacksThe measurement counts only one of themKeep one stack per item, as the stackable inventory guide does, or add the quantities of the two stacks in the scan.
The save of the recipes failsThe pack may already be writtenKeep the confirmation in On JSON Saved to File, and write the two documents in one file when they must stay consistent.
The game runs on HTML5Apppath$ has no meaningUse a browser reference such as storage://crafting and read the platform-specific file guide.
The game runs on Android or iOSThe files land in the sandbox of the applicationUse the path documented for that target and test the export.

Two rules deserve emphasis. First, the craft checks every ingredient before it consumes anything, so a failed craft never eats the materials of the hero. Second, the working tables are rebuilt at every craft, and they are never a source of truth: the pack of the hero is the source of truth.

K. Runtime Compatibility​

The system relies on four groups of features: file operations, loops over an object, writes with a dynamic key, and arithmetic on the members of an object.

TargetFile workLoops and writesNotes
WindowsImplemented and testedImplemented and testedApppath$ resolves to the folder of the exported application.
AndroidImplemented, not verified by an automated checkImplemented, not verified by an automated checkWrites inside the sandbox of the application. Test the export.
HTML5Implemented and testedImplemented and testedUse a browser reference. storage:// uses the origin storage, download:// dispatches a download, embedded:// is read-only.
macOSUnknownUnknownNo separate build was verified.
iOSImplemented, not verified by an automated checkImplemented, not verified by an automated checkWrites inside the sandbox of the application. Test the export.

A second difference matters for the working tables. A member that does not exist reads as zero on the native runtime and is reported as absent by the HTML5 runtime, so the existence test of Step 8 is written to give the same answer on both targets. The full matrix is on the runtime compatibility page.

M. Next Steps​

  • Add a cost to a recipe. A member gold of the recipe is compared with the purse of the hero before the craft, and subtracted from it when the craft goes through.
  • Show the materials that are missing. The shortage is found in the loop of Step 8, and the identifier of the ingredient is already in the global variable Ingredient Id when the loop stops. Write it to a label and the player sees which material to gather.
  • Require a skill. A member skill of the recipe is compared with the level of the hero, as the equipment guide does with the requirement of a piece.
  • Add a station. A member station of the recipe names the object of the level that the player must be close to, and the craft button is only active next to it.
  • Remove the empty stacks. A scan that collects the paths of the stacks with a quantity of zero and deletes them from the last to the first keeps the pack clean without breaking the paths of the working table.
  • Keep the working tables out of the file. Delete /materials and /material_paths when the craft is over, so the saved document only holds the pack.
  • Protect the recipes. The extension can validate the file of the recipes against a JSON schema, so a recipe that lost a member is reported instead of ignored. See Enable JSON Schema Validation.
